Video-driven skeletal animation of arbitrary 3D assets

Anonymous authors

Give Ani4D a rigged 3D asset and a video of something moving. It poses the asset's own skeleton in every frame and drives the mesh by linear blend skinning, so parts stay rigid and bones keep their length — for characters, animals, creatures and articulated objects, in rendered and in-the-wild videos. Abstract

TL;DRAni4D animates any rigged 3D asset from a video by posing its own skeleton, instead of moving its vertices. A 3D generator pretrained to rig static assets finds that skeleton in every frame, so even unseen characters, animals and objects move by their bones without stretching.

Reconstructing an animatable 3D asset from a given video is a fundamental task in 3D content creation. Recent feed-forward video-to-4D methods efficiently reconstruct motion through vertex-based deformation, but often distort rigid parts, stretch geometry, and fuse nearby parts. Skeletal animation offers a structured alternative, yet learning it across diverse objects remains challenging due to varying skeleton topologies and scarce animated 3D data.

Our key insight is that a rigged image-to-3D model, pretrained on large collections of static assets, already provides generalisable priors over geometry and articulation that can be leveraged for video-conditioned skeletal animation. Building on these priors, our model predicts posed geometry and skeletons across video frames. We then recover joint transformations on the asset's own kinematic tree and apply linear blend skinning, producing structurally consistent animations. When no asset is given, an off-the-shelf image-to-3D generator and rigger supply one from the first frame, so that a video alone yields a rigged, animatable asset together with its skeletal motion.

To overcome 4D data scarcity, we develop an agentic pipeline that curates rigged assets and automatically creates and validates motions, yielding 15,357 animation clips (4,436 with generated motion) rendered from 16 viewpoints. On our meticulously curated benchmark of 50 test clips from Truebones Zoo and Objaverse(-XL) with unseen skeletons, each filmed from four viewpoints, our method outperforms prior video-to-4D approaches under various metrics while better preserving part rigidity than vertex-based methods.

Method

Generate posed skeletons, then read out the rig's pose

Ani4D exploits the prior of a pretrained rigged image-to-3D model whose skeleton is a set of voxels inside the object. Every frame is represented the way that model represents a static asset, and attention also reaches the neighbouring frames and the first frame, which holds the input asset.

Stage 1 · where

Posed geometry per frame

A flow model generates each frame's occupancy: the surface voxels and the skeleton voxels, in that frame's own normalised cube, attending to neighbouring frames and to the input asset in frame 0.

Stage 2 · what

Skeletons that name their joints

A second flow fills the voxels with the pretrained model's shape and skeleton latents plus a joint identity, so every skeleton voxel says which joint of the input rig it belongs to. A root token carries the global motion.

Read-out · how

Joint transforms on the asset's own tree

A deterministic fit turns the generated skeletons into rotations of the input rig's joints. Linear blend skinning then poses the input mesh: rigid parts stay rigid and bones keep their length by construction.

Agentic data generation

Curated assets, and motion generated by agents

Rigged, animated 3D assets are scarce and dominated by walk, run and idle cycles. We curate existing animated assets with rule-based audits, a vision–language model and human review, then generate new motion on their own rigs in an actor–critic loop: a language model plans and keyframes actions, a geometric critic and a vision–language judge accept or return each clip.
