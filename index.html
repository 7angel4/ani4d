Given a video and, optionally, a rigged 3D asset, Ani4D reconstructs skeletal motion: it poses the asset's own skeleton in every frame and drives the mesh by linear blend skinning, so parts stay rigid and bones keep their length — for characters, animals, creatures and articulated objects. Abstract

TL;DRAni4D reconstructs an animatable 4D mesh from a video by posing a rigged asset's own skeleton instead of moving its vertices. A rigged image-to-3D model pretrained on static assets predicts that skeleton in every frame, so even unseen characters, animals and objects move by their bones without stretching; with no asset given, one is generated from the first frame.

Reconstructing animatable 3D assets from video is fundamental to 3D content creation. Recent feed-forward video-to-4D methods efficiently reconstruct motion through vertex-based deformation, but often distort rigid parts, stretch geometry, and fuse nearby parts. Skeletal animation offers a structured alternative, yet diverse skeleton topologies and scarce animated 3D data make learning across objects challenging.

Our key insight is that rigged image-to-3D models pretrained on large-scale static data provide generalisable geometric and articulation priors useful for video-conditioned skeletal animation. Building on these priors, we introduce Ani4D, which predicts posed geometry and skeletons across frames, then recovers joint transformations on the asset's kinematic tree and applies linear blend skinning to produce structurally consistent animations. When no asset is given, an off-the-shelf generator and rigger produce one from the first frame, enabling video-only reconstruction of a rigged asset and its motion.

To overcome 4D data scarcity, we develop an agentic pipeline that curates existing animations and generates and validates new motions, yielding 15,100 training clips (4,400 agent-generated). On both our AniBench benchmark of 50 clips with unseen skeletons and four viewpoints each, and the existing MeshBench in the video-only setting, our method outperforms prior video-to-4D approaches across multiple metrics while better preserving part rigidity.

Method

Two temporal flows and a deterministic pose read-out

Ani4D builds on AniGen, a pretrained rigged image-to-3D model that represents an object's skeleton as a set of voxels inside the object. It keeps AniGen's two stages, initialised from its pretrained flows, and adapts them to video with temporal attention to the neighbouring frames and to frame 0, which holds the input asset.

Stage 1 · sparse-structure flow

Posed geometry per frame

A flow model generates each frame's occupancy: the surface voxels and the skeleton voxels, in that frame's own bounding cube. Each frame attends to the frames within two of it and to frame 0, which holds the input asset, kept clean as a fixed anchor.

Stage 2 · structured-latent flow

Skeletons that name their joints

A second flow fills the voxels with the pretrained model's shape, skinning and skeleton latents. Each skeleton voxel also carries a 9-number joint identity, which says which joint of the input rig it belongs to, and a root token per frame carries the root's translation and rotation relative to frame 0. We train the self-attention layers, the skeleton stream and the new layers.

Read-out · closed form

Joint transforms on the asset's own tree

A deterministic read-out turns each posed skeleton into a pose of the input rig in five steps, without optimisation: voxel votes, their assignment to input joints by tags and tracking, joint positions, joint rotations in closed form with temporal smoothing, and linear blend skinning. Rigid parts stay rigid and bones keep their length by construction. Optionally, shape optimisation refines the rotations towards stage 2's per-frame shapes.

In the wild · DAVIS and Consistent4D

Video-only reconstruction in the wild

An in-the-wild video comes with no 3D asset. TRELLIS.2 generates a mesh from the first video frame and SkinTokens its skeleton and skinning weights, keeping the best of six candidates by first-frame silhouette overlap; Ani4D then animates it as a given asset. With such generated assets, Ani4D has lower Chamfer errors and MPJPE than every baseline on AniBench, and lower CD-4D, CD-M and MPJPE on MeshBench (quantitative comparison below). Cards show the input video, our animated asset with its skeleton, and a swinging camera.

Agentic data generation

Curated assets, and motion generated by agents

Rigged, animated 3D assets are scarce, noisy and dominated by walk, run and idle cycles. We curate collected animations with rule-based audits, vision–language review and human review, keeping 10,700 of 85,872 candidate clips. We then generate new motion on their own rigs in an actor–critic loop with a separate judge: a vision–language model plans actions and keyframes joint rotations, a geometric critic checks each clip in 3D, a vision–language judge must recognise its action among 12 labels, and failed clips are repaired or revised.
